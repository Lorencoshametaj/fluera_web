The meta-analysis is honest about size, which is why it is worth citing. Typing wins on volume by a wide margin — typed notes hold far more words. Handwriting wins on the thing notes exist for: achievement, at Hedges' g = 0.248 (p < 0.001) — small, and reliably there.

It also replaces a citation we used to lean on. For ten years the standard reference was Mueller and Oppenheimer (2014), which reported a much larger effect and then failed two replications — Morehead, Dunlosky and Rawson in 2019, and Urry and colleagues in 2021. Those failures did not disprove the finding; an effect this small is invisible to a single study of 142 people. But a claim is only as strong as the evidence a reader finds when they check, so we cite the meta-analysis and its real number.

One moderator matters more to Fluera than the headline. Test delay changed nothing. Factual versus conceptual questions changed nothing. But the chance to **review** the notes before assessment made the handwriting advantage larger. The value of writing by hand is not spent at the moment of writing — it is collected later, when you come back. That is the moment Fluera is built around.
