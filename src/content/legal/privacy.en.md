## 2. Personal data processed

Fluera processes the following categories of personal data, strictly as needed for the declared purposes and subject to granular consent where required.

### 2.1 Account data
- Email address if you create an account (Supabase Auth)
- User identifier (`user_id`) generated by Supabase — also for anonymous sessions
- Auth provider data if used (Google Sign-In, Sign in with Apple): email, name, unique provider ID

### 2.2 User-generated content
- Canvases, strokes, notes, PDF annotations, inserted images
- Audio recordings of study sessions (only if Time Travel is enabled)
- Content is stored in a local database scoped to your account. The on-device database is **encrypted at rest with ChaCha20-Poly1305** (Art. 32 GDPR) using a 256-bit key generated on your device and held in your operating system's keystore — iOS/macOS Keychain, Android Keystore, libsecret on Linux, DPAPI on Windows. We never see the key. So that a new phone does not cost you your canvases, the key has a copy in the **encrypted system backup**, next to the copy of the database the system already makes: on Android in Google Play services Block Store, saved to your Google account only when that backup is end-to-end encrypted with your phone's screen lock (Android 9 or later with a PIN, pattern or password), otherwise on the phone only; on iOS in the encrypted iCloud or Finder backup, with the rest of the Keychain (not in iCloud Keychain). Content leaves your device in three cases, each on your own action: when you enable **Cloud Sync**; when you **publish** a study card to the catalogue; when you keep a card in your **private catalogue** to share it by link. The last two work even with Cloud Sync off, and cover only the portion of notes you select — never the whole canvas. See §2.11
- **Photos, PDFs, audio and history:** inserted photos and images, imported PDFs, audio recordings and the canvas history (Time Travel) are not in that database: they are separate files in the app's folders. Fluera does not encrypt them with the database key; the operating system protects them, by keeping the app's folder closed to other apps and, when it is on, with the device's own encryption.
- **What that means if you change device or reinstall:** if you restore a system backup (a new phone, a phone restore or, on Android with Google backup on, reinstalling the app), the database and its key come back together and your canvases reopen on their own, as they were at the system's last backup. They do not come back if the system backup is off; on Android without Google Play services, or in the Google backup without a screen lock; if you clear the app's data, or uninstall and reinstall without a restore (on iOS the database does not survive uninstalling); on Android if the app's data exceeds the 25 MB Google backup limit, because the system then backs up none of it. In those cases the local database stays unreadable — to you and to us alike — and **Cloud Sync is the recovery path**, which is opt-in: if you have not enabled it, your canvases exist only on that device.
- **Beta note:** databases created before encryption reached the app (August 2026) are unencrypted and cannot be opened with a key. The first time a version with encryption starts, such a database is **deleted** and a new encrypted one is created. Canvases already synced to the cloud come back; canvases that only ever existed locally do not.

### 2.3 Usage data (telemetry)
Only if you have consented to the **Product analytics** category:
- Randomly generated anonymous `session_id` per session
- Your account identifier, so that you can access and delete your own telemetry (§6). The crash-reporting processor (§5) receives only a SHA-256 hash of it, never the plaintext identifier
- Platform, app version, subscription tier, device language
- Product events from a server-side whitelist (session start/end, Ghost Map, Socratic, SRS reviews, AI calls with duration and tokens)
- **How much you remember of a catalogue card:** if you install a card from the catalogue and reopen its canvas after 1, 7 and 30 days, each time the app sends a single number: how far your memory of its concepts, computed on the device, sits above what would remain without studying it. With the number travel the card's identifier, the day (1, 7 or 30) and the formula version. It is linked to your account, including the anonymous session. In the catalogue nobody sees it on its own: it only counts in the average of at least 5 real accounts, to show which cards people really learn from. It is not sent if telemetry is off or cognitive memory is off (§2.9), and the server discards it if the Marketplace signal is off (§2.11). It stays until you withdraw the Analytics consent, turn the Marketplace signal off, use "Delete all my data from the cloud" or delete the account
- **Not collected:** note content, question text, identifying information.

### 2.4 AI feature data
Only if you have consented to the **AI features** category:
- Selected canvas content sent for Socratic Mode, Ghost Map, LaTeX OCR, Exam Session — text portions and rendered PNG images of individual handwriting-cluster regions (never the full notebook)
- AI inference runs on Google Vertex AI in the **European Union**, on EU endpoints only: the europe-west4 (Netherlands) and europe-west1 (Belgium) regions, or the Vertex AI EU multi-region endpoint, which Google limits to EU member states; canvas content is not transferred outside the EU for AI inference
- Usage tokens for plan limit enforcement
- In production, calls go through a Supabase Edge Function proxy (API key server-side)

### 2.5 Cloud Sync data
Only if you have consented to the **Cloud Sync** category:
- Canvas copies stored on Supabase (EU region `eu-north-1`), encrypted in transit (TLS) and at rest at the infrastructure level. Cloud Sync is **not** end-to-end encrypted: as the data controller, Fluera can technically access synced content to provide, secure and support the service (we never sell it or use it for advertising)
- **Browser folds are the exception.** If you turn on "Browser folds" in Settings → Data, the pages folded into a canvas (addresses, quotes, page copies, the back of the page, threads, ink tied to the minute) reach your other devices **end-to-end encrypted**: sealed on the device with AES-256-GCM and a key born on the device that the server never receives. The key reaches a new device of yours through a pairing that shows the same 6-digit code on both, or through the recovery code you see once when you turn it on; the server keeps only a copy sealed with that code. The server sees which canvas each fold belongs to, its type and when it changes, never its content. Fludomi's history, favorites, site preferences and open tabs travel with the same key, unless you stop them in "What travels" (§2.14). If you lose all your devices and the recovery code, those folds cannot be recovered (your canvases can). "Turn off on all devices" deletes them from the server
- Sync metadata (hash, timestamp, size)

**Sync across your devices** *(Cloud plan; on the Free plan up to 500 MB, without recorded lessons)*. In *Settings → Data and sync* there is the switch "Sync across your devices". It is off until you turn it on. It applies to your whole account: on for one device means on for every device you sign in on. To turn it on you need an account (not a guest session) and the Cloud plan. When you turn it on, the server records the date and the version of this policy you had in front of you.

- **What goes to the cloud, only while the switch is on:** whole canvases (strokes, sections, formulas, tables, charts, links, PDFs with the marks you made in the reader, photos); recorded lessons (the audio, in pieces, with the strokes tied to the audio); the study of each canvas (review cards with the history of your answers, concepts, exams with their ink, the Socratic tutor, the chat, recall, the concept map, courses); the gallery's folders (name, colour, parent folder, pinned), each canvas's folder, the pinned canvases, the labels and the Trash (which canvases are in it, and since when); the text Fluera recognised in your handwriting, with your corrections; your choices in the settings (appearance, language, paper and export format, gallery order, review, exams and reminders, colours, eraser, recently used formulas, browser settings, tool groups of the pencil case). Settings tied to a single device (hand, palm, pen, window) stay on the device.
- **What stays on the device even with the switch on:** the canvas history (Time Travel) and the undo and redo stack.
- **Note-group titles across your devices.** When you name a group of notes, that name is text you wrote. With the switch on it goes to the cloud together with a reference to the strokes it belongs to and the time you changed it, so the most recent name wins even if you edit it on two devices.
- **Who sees this data:** you. On a canvas you share with other people, its members see the canvas and the note-group titles; your study (cards, answers, exams, chat) is visible to you alone.
- **The device name.** To tell you where a canvas is open ("Open on Redmi 15 5G") and to bring the folds key to another device, the app sends the server the name the device has in its system settings (on a computer, its name), together with a random code created at installation. If that name contains your name ("Mario's phone"), that is sent too. Only you see it, on the devices of your account. It stays on the server only as long as it is needed: a canvas's turn lasts a few minutes and is renewed while the canvas is open; a pairing request expires after 15 minutes. An expired row may remain until that canvas is next opened or until the next pairing, and it goes with the account. We do not send the serial number or any other device code.
- **Where and how:** on Supabase, in the EU region `eu-north-1` (Stockholm), like the rest of Cloud Sync: encrypted in transit (TLS) and at rest at the infrastructure level, **not** end-to-end encrypted. As the data controller, Fluera can technically access it to provide, secure and support the service; we never sell it or use it for advertising.
- **Turning it off:** you can turn the switch off from any device, and it stops on all of them. It also turns off if you withdraw the **Cloud Sync** consent in *Settings → Privacy and telemetry*. If the Cloud plan ends, syncing pauses. In all three cases **nothing is deleted**: what is already in the cloud stays there until you delete it, except for the §6 rule for a Cloud plan closed for more than 6 months, and everything stays on your devices.
- **Without the switch** this data stays on the device, as before: canvases, lessons, study, cognitive memory (§2.9), recognised text and settings. Of this data, only what you send leaves: a canvas you share with other people, catalogue cards (§2.11), requests to the AI features (§2.4).

### 2.8 Third-party software components (on-device)

Fluera includes some embedded third-party SDKs that process data **exclusively on your device**, without sending anything back to their producers. For transparency:

- **Google ML Kit** (on-device Digital Ink & Text Recognition) — on-device handwriting and text/image recognition. Recognition runs locally on your device; ink and images are not sent to Google for these on-device APIs (recognition models are downloaded from Google). Google receives no canvas content from Fluera via ML Kit.
- **sqlite3mc** — the local database engine, which provides the ChaCha20-Poly1305 encryption described in §2.2. The key is supplied by the app from your device's keystore; sqlite3mc itself sends nothing anywhere. All processing is on-device.
- **Sentry SDK** (Functional Software Inc.) — only the embedded library; see §4 for the data controller and §3 for the legal basis of crash report transfer (consent, opt-in).

These components are **not data processors** under Art. 28 GDPR because they do not receive personal data. They are third-party **software components** like any other library used to build the app.

### 2.9 Cognitive memory (on-device indexing)

On by default (opt-out). Fluera builds **on your device** a cognitive index of your notes that powers the study features — automatic cluster titles, the concept map ("Ghost Map"), spaced repetition (FSRS), and the learning-state checkpoints ("Sé"). The index is always built and computed on the device.

- **Where it stays:** on your device. It leaves only if you turn on "Sync across your devices" (§2.5, Cloud plan): then your review cards with the history of your answers, your concepts, the concept map and the note-group titles go through Fluera's cloud to reach your other devices. Without the switch the index stays on the device, as before. It is distinct from *AI features* (§2.4), which remain a separate consent.
- **Derived data:** cluster and concept identifiers, on-device-generated titles, the concept-graph structure, the concept event log, repetition scheduling. On the device they are stored under the same protection described in §2.2; the copy in the cloud, if you turn sync on, as described in §2.5.
- **Control (opt-out):** you can disable it at any time from *Settings → Privacy → Cognitive memory*. On disable, indexing stops and **the cognitive data already created on this device is erased immediately**; your notes stay intact. If you use Sync across your devices, the copies in the cloud are deleted too (review cards with their answers, concepts, the concept map and note-group titles), and none goes up again from any of your devices until you re-enable it; on your other devices the copies stay until you disable it there too. Re-enabling rebuilds the index.

### 2.10 Handwriting recognition dataset (opt-in, off by default)

Fluera is building its own handwriting recogniser trained on real handwriting
from Fluera users. **Every collection mode is off by default**, each is a
separate decision, and each is revocable at any time from *Settings → Privacy →
AI Training Data*.

- **What is collected:** raw strokes (coordinates, timing, pressure, stylus
  tilt where supported), a small PNG image of each handwriting cluster, and
  basic device information (platform, pointer type). Never your name, your
  email, or the content of PDFs you read.
- **How it is protected:** encrypted on your device with AES-256-GCM **before**
  upload. Only a pseudonymous identifier travels with it — never your name or
  email — so we cannot link a contribution back to your account from the data
  itself.
- **Where it goes:** Supabase, EU region `eu-north-1` (Stockholm), the same as
  §2.5. When our Vertex AI agreement is finalised, Google Gemini will analyse
  the images to generate training labels; until then nothing is sent to Google.
- **Legal basis:** explicit and revocable consent (Art. 6.1.a GDPR), separate
  from the *AI features* consent of §2.4.
- **Retention:** up to 5 years after account deletion, or until you withdraw it —
  whichever comes first, always pseudonymised. This is the one category that
  deliberately outlives your account: because contributions are pseudonymised
  before upload, deleting your account does not reach them.
- **How to withdraw, and the limit you should know about (Art. 11 GDPR):** the
  pseudonym attached to your contributions is computed on your device, from a
  key that never leaves it. That is what makes the dataset genuinely
  unlinkable to your account — and it has a consequence we would rather state
  than hide. *Settings → Privacy → AI Training Data* erases the contributions
  made **from that device**, as long as the app's data is still there. If you
  uninstall the app, clear its data, or move to a new device, that key is gone,
  and neither you nor we can any longer tell which contributions were yours. In
  that case we are not in a position to identify you within this dataset
  (Art. 11 GDPR), and what bounds the data is the 5-year retention above.
  If you plan to withdraw, do it **before** uninstalling. Contributions made
  from a different device must be withdrawn from that device.

### 2.11 Catalogue: published cards and private cards

Fluera lets you turn a portion of your notes into a **study card** and share it.
It is always your explicit action: without it, nothing you write leaves the
device by this route. There are two destinations, and the difference between
them is the whole point.

- **Public catalogue** — the card becomes visible to anyone, indexable by search
  engines and reachable from a public share page.
- **Private catalogue** — the card stays yours and reaches **only the people you
  send a link to**. It appears in no search, has no public page, and the link's
  social preview shows no content: someone who receives it in a group chat sees
  only "somebody shared a card with you".

**What is uploaded.** The card's bytes (the portion of notes you selected, plus
the list of concepts we derive from it), a **PNG thumbnail** — an image of the
handwritten page —, the title you write, the concept count, the size, a SHA-256
digest of the content, the version of the Terms you accept, and your account
identifier. Your **learner model** (what you remember, when you review) is
**not** uploaded: it stays on the device.

**Why the thumbnail is mandatory.** It is what the person receiving the link
looks at before deciding whether to install, and the only thing a moderator can
see if the card is reported. Without it, a removal would be decided blind.

**Who can see it.**

- **You**, always.
- **The people who open your link.** They become recipients of the data the card
  contains: it is a disclosure to third parties, and you decide it.
- **An automated check** on upload, which analyses the thumbnail and the images
  inside the card to catch unlawful content. It runs at Google Cloud (Vertex AI)
  inside the European Union — see §4 and §5.
- **A human moderator**, and **only** if the card has been reported. In that case
  they see the thumbnail, never the card's bytes. There is no way to open a
  private card nobody has reported.

**The link.** It has an **expiry** (72 hours by default, never beyond 7 days) and
a **cap on people** (25 by default). You can revoke it at any time, and you can
remove one person's access without touching the others. We keep only a
cryptographic fingerprint of the link, never its text: that is why we cannot show
it to you again, and "create a new link" is the only route.

**What revocation does NOT reach, plainly.** Anyone who already **installed** the
card holds a copy on their own device, and that copy stays. A delivered copy
cannot be recalled — not by you, not by us. Revoking prevents new access; it does
not erase what has already been delivered.

**Who sent you a card, and who received it.** The author sees **how many** people
opened the link and **when**, never who they are: we do not return their
identifier. The recipient, in turn, does not receive the author's identifier.

**Retention.** A card stays until you remove it or moderation removes it. Expired
links and revoked authorisations stay recorded so it remains possible to show who
had access and when. On account deletion, the bytes of cards you **authored** are
deleted from our servers along with everything else; cards you merely received
are untouched, because they belong to whoever wrote them.

**Your rights over this feature.** At any time you can revoke a link, remove a
person's access, leave a card you received, remove a card of your own, and report
a card you received. Cards you authored are included in your data export (§7).

**Your creator profile photo.** If you add a photo to your creator profile in the
catalogue, we upload it to Supabase, in the EU (§4), and it goes through an
**automated check** like the one for cards, which runs at Google Cloud
(Vertex AI) inside the European Union — see §4 and §5. The check receives only
the image, without your name or any other profile data. The purpose is the same: keeping unlawful content
out of the catalogue (§3). That is why it happens even if you have not given
consent to `AI features`: that consent covers the study features (§2.4), not
this check.

- **Until the check has approved it**, we show the photo to no one but you. Once
  approved, it is visible to anyone who opens your creator profile.
- **If the check does not approve it**, we remove it from your profile and delete
  the file. Only if it detects child sexual abuse material do we keep a
  restricted copy, as evidence for the authorities.
- **You can remove or change it whenever you like**: the old photo stops being
  shown at once. If you change it, the old photo's file is deleted once the new
  one is approved. If you remove it, or the new one is not approved, the old
  file stays on our servers until we approve a new photo of yours or you delete
  your account: we no longer show it, but anyone who already has its address can
  still open it.

**The marketplace signal.** When you install a card from the catalogue, the app
tells our server, and also tells it if you come back to study it. This is used
to count which cards people install and which ones they come back to, and so to
recognise the creators whose cards are genuinely useful. It is on by default and
you turn it off in *Settings → Privacy and telemetry → Marketplace signal*.

- **What travels:** the identifier of the installed card and, if you come back
  to study it (a completed review, or reopening it at least 20 hours after
  installing it), the fact that you came back. It travels tied to your account,
  including the anonymous session the app opens if you have not signed in. The
  server adds the time of the install and of the return.
- **What does not travel:** your name, your email, the content of the card or
  of your notes, how much you remember (that travels only with Analytics, §2.3).
- **What it is used for:** only installs by real accounts count, not those of
  anonymous sessions. They count in the number of installs shown on the card,
  in the catalogue order (popular, trending and most effective cards) and in
  the creator's figures (the installs on their profile and their badges). The
  same row is used for rating: the server accepts a rating of a card only from
  someone who installed it.
- **On the device** the app keeps the list of installed cards, with the time,
  to notice the return. A card leaves the list when you come back to study it
  or after 30 days.
- **Who sees it:** us, at Supabase in the EU (§4), and you: your rows and the
  switch are in your data export. Creators and other people see only totals, never who
  installed.
- **Turning it off:** the switch applies to your account, on all your
  devices. The server records when you turned it off, with the version of this
  policy, and from then on refuses your account's installs and returns,
  including those of an earlier version of the app that does not know the
  switch. Turning it off deletes from the server all the installs of your
  account, from any device, and the measures of how much you remember of those
  cards (§2.3); the cards' install numbers and the creators' figures are
  recalculated without them. If the device is offline, it stops sending at
  once, and the switch-off reaches the server by itself the next time you open
  a canvas. If you had turned it off with an earlier version of the app, which
  kept it only on the device, and the server has no choice of yours yet, the
  first time the app reads your account from that device it turns it off on
  the server too. If you turn it back on, it
  starts again from zero. "Delete all my data from the cloud" performs the same
  deletions without turning it off, and "Delete account" performs them before
  deleting the account.
- **If you rate with the signal off:** the server accepts a rating only from
  someone who installed the card, so the app records that you installed it, in
  a separate row that does not count: nothing in the install number, the
  creators' figures or the catalogue order. If you later turn the signal back
  on, that row still does not count. From an earlier version of the app, with
  the signal off, the rating goes through only if that row already exists.
- **The device code:** earlier versions of the app also sent a random 128-bit
  code created on the device, without an account. The server no longer
  accepts it and the app no longer sends it. The remaining rows delete
  themselves 30 days after the install, or earlier if from that device you
  turn the signal off or use one of the two deletions above; the device then
  forgets the code.

### 2.12 Study digest for your connected AI assistant (opt-in, off by default)

If you enable "Connected AI assistant", Fluera publishes a compact **study
digest** to our server so that an AI assistant YOU connect can read your study
state through the Model Context Protocol.

- **What it contains, and nothing else:** only course names, exam dates and outcomes, readiness as counts, the titles of concepts currently due for review with their date, stage, durability in days according to the model and how many times they have already lapsed, the titles of concepts you have never studied, the CONCEPT on which you have a correction still to recheck together with its recheck date, and — where available — weak topics as coarse bands, how many topics have passed the check that precedes a closed-book test and how many have not, and for those that have not the title and the REASON that is missing: too few demanding questions, a single session, sessions too close together, no successful recall after a pause, too many recent errors, a memory gone cold, competence still below threshold, or an answer that felt right and was not, still to be reviewed. That reason is a technical label chosen by Fluera from eight possible ones — never a sentence of yours.
- **What it never contains:** your notes, handwriting, OCR text, images, and the content of your error journal — the sentence you wrote, the correction and the critique — are not included: all that leaves the journal is the concept it belongs to and the recheck date. A "counts only" switch (Settings → Cognitive features) removes concept titles as well.
- **Where it is stored:** on Supabase in the EU region `eu-north-1`, one row per course, overwritten in place. It is included in your data export (§7).
- **How long:** only as long as you keep the feature on. Revoking this consent deletes the stored digest; deleting a course or a canvas deletes its row; deleting your account deletes everything.
- **Who can read it:** only an assistant holding a personal connector token that you create in the app and can revoke at any time. Revoking this consent also stops every token from resolving.
- **Legal basis:** consent, Art. 6(1)(a), withdrawable at any time from Settings → Privacy.

The assistant you connect is chosen and authorised by you; its provider
processes what it reads under its own terms (see §4, "Assistants you
connect"). Fluera does not send your digest to any AI provider on its own
initiative.

### 2.13 Visitors to the website and the web catalogue

This section is about anyone who visits **fluera.dev** or **share.fluera.dev**
in a browser, even without a Fluera account.

- **Technical logs of the hosting providers.** fluera.dev is served by GitHub
  (GitHub Pages), share.fluera.dev by Deno (Deno Deploy). Like any web server,
  they record for each request the IP address, the time, the page requested
  and the browser's identifier, to run and protect the service. The providers
  keep them under their own rules (§4); Fluera does not use them to identify
  or profile you.
- **No cookies of our own, no analytics.** The pages set no cookies of our own
  and load no analytics or tracking tools. fluera.dev remembers in your
  browser only your light or dark theme and whether you closed the language
  notice: this stays on your device and is not sent to us.
- **Catalogue images.** Card previews are served by Supabase through
  Cloudflare's network, which may set the technical cookie `__cf_bm` (bot
  protection, 30 minutes). It is a technical cookie of the provider, needed by
  the service and not used to profile you.
- **Invite links.** When someone opens an author's invite link
  (share.fluera.dev/i/…), we count the click for that code, with the platform
  (for example "Android") and the time: no data about who clicked.
- **Report form** (share.fluera.dev/report). If you report content we process
  what you write in the form — the content reported, the reason, the
  description, the good-faith statement and, if you give them, name and email
  (required for copyright reports, optional for the others) — to handle the
  report and reply to you, as Article 16 of Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 ("DSA")
  requires. To stop automated submissions, the number of recent submissions
  per IP address is kept only in the server's memory, for 10 minutes.
- **Legal bases:** legitimate interest in running and securing the service for
  logs, clicks and abuse protection (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR); legal obligation for
  reports (Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR, DSA).
- **Retention:** logs stay with the providers for the period set by their
  rules; reports as moderation decisions (§6).

### 2.14 Fludomi, the built-in browser

Fludomi opens web pages **directly from your device**: our servers do not see the pages you visit
or what you search for.

- **The sites you visit** receive what any site receives from a browser: your IP address, the
  browser type (in a reduced form, with no device model), your language, and the cookies that site
  has set. They are independent controllers of the data they collect, under their own policies.
- **When you write on the page with the pen**, the site does not receive your strokes: it receives
  only the touches Fludomi passes on to it (a click, a control you drag, a frame of another site
  embedded in the page). Nor does it receive where the hovering pen moves. On iPad two limits
  remain: the site can still know which element of the page the hovering Pencil is over (the
  «:hover» style), and a frame the page fills by itself, without loading it from an address, can
  still receive the Pencil, the start of strokes included.
- **The search engine** you choose in Settings (default: DuckDuckGo) receives what you search for
  and your IP address. «Search in Fludomi» also sends it the text you selected in a note or PDF.
- **Google Lens**, only when you use it, receives the image you choose (a PDF crop, an image from
  a note or a page) and your IP address. Lens runs in a separate space: only Google's consent is
  kept there, never the sign-in to your Google account used for browsing.
- **Protection from dangerous sites** checks addresses before opening them. On Android this is
  Google Safe Browsing: the service receives only hashed fragments of addresses. On iPhone, iPad
  and Mac it is Apple's fraudulent-website warning. It is on by default and can be turned off in
  Fludomi's settings («Safe Browsing»).
- **On Windows and Linux there is no protection from dangerous sites**: Fludomi does not check
  addresses with a dangerous-sites service. On Windows the system's one (Microsoft SmartScreen)
  would receive the full address of every page, and we keep it off; on Linux there is none.
- **The lookalike-site warning**, on every device: if a page's address imitates a well-known
  site's (for example «paypa1.com», or letters from another alphabet that look like those of
  «paypal.com»), Fludomi stops the page and shows a warning in its place. The check runs on the
  device, with lists that are inside the app, and sends nothing to anyone.
- **Handwriting** in the address bar is recognised on the device. The language model is
  downloaded once from Google's servers.

**What stays on the device.** These stay in your account's encrypted database (§2.2):
- your history, deleted automatically after the period you choose (1 day, 1 week, 1 month or
  3 months, the default) without a visit; it can also be turned off;
- favourites and open tabs;
- the icons of the sites you visit, deleted with the history;
- highlights, folds, sources and the text copy of the pages you fold;
- per-site preferences.

Cookies and the data sites store (sign-ins, preferences) live in the browser space of your
account:
- they are excluded from Google and iCloud backups;
- they are deleted by «Clear browsing data»;
- they are deleted when you sign out or delete your account;
- they are deleted for a single site with «Forget this site»;
- if you turn on «Clear everything on close», they are deleted every time you close Fludomi or
  Fluera, together with the history and the open tabs.

If you delete your account, its local database is deleted from the device too.

**Private tabs.** They save no history, tabs or folds, and on Android the keyboard does not
learn what you type in them. Their cookies are deleted when they close, or at the next launch if the app was
killed. They close by themselves after 15 minutes with Fluera in the background. The system keeps no picture of the tab in the recent-apps list.

**Protections on by default:**
- we block the most common tracking services, with a list we write ourselves;
- no third-party cookies;
- cookie banners are refused, never accepted, when you have not answered yet;
- every site receives the Global Privacy Control signal (do not sell or share my data);
- we remove tracking parameters from addresses (for example `utm_`, `fbclid`, `gclid`);
- we open links over https whenever the site offers it; a site without https opens only after
  a warning, and on Android in the phone's browser.

If you turn sync on, your folds, history, favorites, the text size and the camera and microphone
blocks chosen for each site, and the tabs open on each device (with its name and their sites' icons) travel between your
devices. They are end-to-end encrypted with the folds key (§2.5): the server sees how many entries
there are, of which type and when they change, never the addresses or the titles. Private tabs,
cookies, sign-ins to sites and the permissions granted to a site do not travel. In "What travels" you can stop history, favorites, site preferences and open tabs one by one. The choice applies to all your devices: the server no longer accepts that type and deletes its copies, while everything stays on your devices, except the list of the other devices' tabs. "Clear browsing
data" also deletes them from the server and, at their next sync, from your other devices; "Clear
everything on close" deletes only on the device where it is on. If you turn sync off, their copies
leave the server; on your devices they stay.

### 2.15 Reports of content written by the AI

Next to content written by the AI (the questions of Socratic mode and of the Exam, the AI's messages in the chat with your notes) there is a flag to report it. A report leaves only if you tap it and confirm, and it leaves even if you have withdrawn the Analytics consent: it is your request, not telemetry. With the report we send:
- the text of the reported question or message, its language and the feature that wrote it;
- the reason, if you write one;
- the app version, your account identifier, the time and a random code of the report, used so that it is not recorded twice if the sending is repeated.

We do not send your answers, your messages or anything else from your notes. Without a connection the report stays on the device, in the app's encrypted database, and leaves as soon as possible; without an account it does not leave. Reports are used to fix wrong or offensive content and to improve what does not sound natural. We read them; no other user sees them, and we accept at most 20 a day per account. You find them in the export of your data.
